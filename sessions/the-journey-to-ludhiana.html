Tuesday, November 26, 2024

The Journey to Ludhiana

Part I

Session 08 of 22 5 min read Road to Qadian

Today is both a sad and an exciting day. Sad, because we are leaving Delhi and saying goodbye to the gracious and incredible hosts at Masjid Baitul Hadi. They have been so accommodating, always serving with smiles and quiet dedication. Words cannot do justice to their kindness. May Allah bless them and their families for their sacrifices and service.

But today is also a day of excitement! We are headed to Qadian! Along the way, we will stop in Ludhiana and Hoshiarpur—two places deeply tied to the history of Ahmadiyyat.

Farewell to Delhi

We departed from Masjid Baitul Hadi at 5 a.m., before Fajr prayers, on a cool Delhi morning. The drive to Ludhiana Railway Station spanned approximately 225 miles (360 km). This railway station holds historical significance as it was used by Hazrat Promised Messiah (as) during his travels from Batala to Ludhiana.

On the Road to Ludhiana

About 90 minutes into the journey, we stopped for breakfast. Much like the previous day, the food was exceptional. We enjoyed channa poori and other dishes, but the star of the meal was the ginger-flavored tea served in mini clay cups—it was absolutely perfect.

After breakfast and some quick shopping, we boarded the buses again. Traffic was a mix of heavy and light, slowing us down as we approached Ludhiana. About 40 minutes from our destination, we were greeted by Punjab Police.

The police escort, arranged under the direct instructions and guidance of Markaz and Hazrat Khalifatul Masih V (aba), was crucial. They guided us through heavy traffic and even a protest where vehicles were at a standstill, likely saving us over an hour of travel time.

Arrival in Ludhiana

As we reached Ludhiana, our buses couldn’t navigate the narrow streets of the old city, so we disembarked near the railway station. This same station was used by Hazrat Promised Messiah (as) during his many trips to Ludhiana.

From there, all 80 of us walked in single file, led by the chaperones of each group. Everyone wore hats with the Jamaat logo and jackets featuring the Waqf-e-Nau insignia, as well as USA and India flags. Punjab Police managed traffic at crossings to ensure our safety, as the bustling streets of Ludhiana are vastly different from what most of us are used to. Passersby and shopkeepers stopped to watch us, many recording videos and taking pictures. It seemed they hadn’t seen such a large, coordinated group before.

As I walked these streets, I couldn’t help but wonder: How did the Promised Messiah (as) navigate these same paths over a century ago? The roads wouldn’t have been paved then—likely soft soil, with standing water here and there. Goats, sheep, and chickens probably roamed freely, and he would have carefully navigated his way through this scene.

The Sacred Site of the First Ba’ait

After weaving through narrow streets, we arrived at our destination: the site where Hazrat Promised Messiah (as) took the first Ba’ait. This is where the journey of Ahmadiyyat began.

The first room to the right as we entered is called Darul Ba’ait. This sacred space is where Hazrat Promised Messiah (as) conducted the first Ba’ait, laying the foundation of Ahmadiyyat. Hazrat Maulana Nooruddin (ra) was the first to pledge allegiance, and the Promised Messiah (as) took his time during this Ba’ait, performing it with great care and devotion.

In front of us is what is now the masjid. However, during the time of the Promised Messiah (as), it was just a raised platform where people gathered to pray and socialize as they waited for their turn to take Ba’ait. This simple setting, filled with devotion and anticipation, marks the birthplace of a movement that now spans the globe.

The local murrabi sahib guided us through the site, explaining its history and significance. The property has been carefully renovated while preserving its original design, with a vibrant orange color that makes it stand out beautifully.

The Journey to Ba’ait

In the years leading up to the first Ba’ait, Sufi Ahmad Jan Sahib and many other prominent Muslims repeatedly urged Hazrat Promised Messiah (as) to begin accepting pledges of allegiance. However, Huzoor (as) always responded that he had not yet received divine permission from Allah to do so.

Sufi Ahmad Jan Sahib’s heartfelt plea was even immortalized in his famous Urdu nazm:

The entire world’s sights are on you;
For the sake of Allah, please be our savior.

These words capture the overwhelming sense of anticipation and hope placed upon the Promised Messiah (as) by those who recognized his divine mission.

Historical Moments

Here are some significant events tied to this sacred place:

  • Hazrat Promised Messiah (as) first came to Ludhiana in 1884 at the invitation of Mir Abbas Ali Sahib and stayed in the house of Sufi Ahmad Jan Sahib, a devout man known for his spiritual practices.
  • On 12 January 1889, Hazrat Promised Messiah (as) finalized the ten conditions of Ba’ait.
  • On 23 March 1889, the first Ba’ait took place in Darul Ba’ait, starting with Hazrat Maulana Nooruddin (ra). Over the following days, many others joined—either in groups, individually, or through letters sent to the Promised Messiah (as). Huzoor (as) stayed in Ludhiana for an extended period, meeting with those eager to join this divine mission.

Preserving the Legacy

When Partition occurred in 1947, this property fell into the hands of a local Hindu resident. Remarkably, they didn’t change anything in the rooms connected to the Promised Messiah (as). After years of legal proceedings, the Jamaat regained the property in 2006.

The first phase of construction began in 2009, with the most recent renovations completed in 2017. The prayer hall we visited wasn’t part of the original property but was added later. At the time of Hazrat Promised Messiah (as), prayers were offered on the raised, open platform with no walls.

Standing in this historic place, I reflected on how far the message of Ahmadiyyat has spread since those first Ba’aits. This is where it all began—a journey that now connects us across the globe.
