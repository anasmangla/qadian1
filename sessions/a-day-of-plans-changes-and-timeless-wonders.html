Chapter 6 of 22

A Day of Plans, Changes, and Timeless Wonders

Sunday, November 24, 2024

1 / 2

Today was a long but exciting day, filled with changes, adjustments, and unexpected detours. Let’s start with the places we were supposed to visit but couldn’t: Tughlakabad Fort and Chandni Chowk. The Tughlakabad system was down, so we were unable to enter, and Chandni Chowk was far too crowded for the admins to safely take the group, especially with younger children. Adding to the challenges, several roads in Delhi were closed due to rallies and protests, forcing us to detour multiple times and prolonging our travel.

These changes reminded us of an important approach to journeys—and life itself:

  • 1. Plan. Plan. Plan.
  • 2. Expect unforeseen changes.
  • 3. Accept changes.

MashaAllah, our Waqf-e-Nau children and their leadership have demonstrated incredible adaptability. Their patience and positive attitudes kept the day enjoyable despite the challenges.

Qutub Minar

Our first stop today was the Qutub Minar, a stunning tower of victory built in 1193 by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, the founder of the Delhi Sultanate. This 73-meter-tall minaret is made of red sandstone and marble, adorned with intricate carvings and verses from the Holy Qur’an. It is surrounded by the Qutub Complex, which includes ancient ruins, mosques, and the Iron Pillar, a marvel of metallurgy that has stood rust-free for centuries.

We explored the courtyards, walked among the ruins, and captured beautiful moments in pictures and videos. The site’s grandeur was awe-inspiring, and we even managed to take a memorable group photo—a keepsake for this once-in-a-lifetime journey.

The Streets of Delhi

After Qutub Minar, we made a long stop by the side of the road as our hosts gathered lunch from local restaurants. While we waited, we had the chance to observe the vibrant life of Delhi unfolding around us. Let me tell you, friends: people-watching is an art, and Delhi is the perfect canvas.

Our very own Mirza Ghalib once said:

This world is like a playground in front of my eyes.
Every day I see a new play in front of me.

Indeed, the scenes were captivating. We saw the struggles of the working poor, laboring tirelessly to make ends meet. We witnessed the joy of wedding processions, colorful and loud, celebrating love amidst life’s chaos. We even saw protestors and marchers advocating for causes, perhaps with varying degrees of understanding. It was a snapshot of humanity in all its forms—raw, dynamic, and endlessly intriguing.

Laal Qila (The Red Fort)

After lunch, and following Zuhr and Asr prayers, we headed to the iconic Red Fort, or Laal Qila. Built in the 17th century by Emperor Shah Jahan, the fort served as the residence of Mughal emperors for nearly 200 years. Its massive red sandstone walls enclose a city within—a collection of palaces, halls, and gardens that speak of the splendor of the Mughal era.

The first sight of the Red Fort is breathtaking. It feels as though time has frozen, and you’ve stepped into the 1600s. We wandered through the barracks and courtyards, trying to imagine life within those walls during its prime. The architecture, with its delicate carvings and grandiose structures, tells stories of power, artistry, and vision.

The Light and Sound Show: 'Jai Hind'

As evening fell, we stayed to watch the renowned light and sound show at the Red Fort, titled Jai Hind. This immersive experience is more than just a spectacle; it’s a journey through 400 years of history, brought to life through state-of-the-art technology, including projection mapping, live-action films, and a rich soundscape. The show narrates key episodes from India’s past, from the Mughal era to the struggle for independence and beyond.

The narration guides the audience through the annals of history, making the experience both educational and emotionally stirring. The use of various art forms, such as puppetry, dance, and live performances, enhances the narrative, creating a multi-sensory journey that captivates everyone.

Even Hazrat Khalifatul Masih (aba) visited this show and thoroughly enjoyed it during his time here. For us, it felt like the past was unfolding right before our eyes—a moving reminder of the rise and fall of empires and the transient nature of worldly power.

Reflections

By the end of the day, those tracking their steps on fitness devices reported walking nearly 10 miles! Exhausted but satisfied, we made our way back to our accommodations. As our eyes grew heavy with sleep, we couldn’t help but wonder: what does tomorrow have in store for us? Only Allah knows, and we leave it in His hands.
