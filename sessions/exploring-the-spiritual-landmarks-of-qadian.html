Thursday, November 28, 2024

Exploring the Spiritual Landmarks of Qadian

Part II

Session 15 of 22 3 min read Qadian

After early morning prayers and a quick breakfast, we gathered for another day of exploring the historic and spiritual landmarks of Qadian. Right on time at 9:30 a.m., our guide, Naseer Haq Acharia Murabbi of Ammore Kharja, arrived with his ever-present smile. He spoke to us in Urdu, and Imam Abdullah Dibba Sahib translated at every stop, ensuring everyone could fully grasp the significance of what we were seeing.

Walking through these places felt like walking through a series of living museums. Each stop carried its own weight of history and spirituality, and we all felt incredibly fortunate to witness these sacred sites in person.

Sacred and Historic Sites

We visited the house of Hazrat Khalifatul Masih I (ra), a simple yet profoundly significant place. Being in the rooms where Hazrat Maulvi Noor-ud-Din (ra) lived and worked was a humbling experience, connecting us to the early days of Khilafat.

The Makhzan-e-Tasweer offered a visual journey through the Jamaat’s history. Its photographs captured moments of sacrifice, progress, and divine blessings that have shaped Ahmadiyyat.

At the Quran Exhibition, we marveled at rare manuscripts and beautifully preserved copies of the Holy Quran. The exhibition showcased the Jamaat’s dedication to the preservation and dissemination of the Quran’s teachings.

The bookstore managed by the Ahmadiyya Nashr-o-Ishaat department was another highlight, featuring an extensive collection of Islamic literature, including the works of the Promised Messiah (as) and his Khulafa.

The Ta‘lim-ul-Islam High School is a testament to the Jamaat’s commitment to education. Its historic buildings have nurtured countless students who have gone on to serve humanity and the Jamaat.

The World Peace Exhibition emphasized the Jamaat’s tireless efforts to promote harmony and understanding globally, conveying Islam’s core message of peace.

We visited Masjid Masroor and Masjid Subhan, serene spaces that radiated peace and provided a beautiful setting for prayer and reflection.

At Sara-i-Tahir, now serving as Jamia Ahmadiyya Qadian, we witnessed the Jamaat’s dedication to training missionaries who spread the true teachings of Islam around the world.

The Noor-ud-Din Library, named after the first Khalifa, housed an extensive collection of Islamic texts and scholarly works, serving as a valuable resource for visitors and researchers.

The Fazl-e-Umar Press showcased the Jamaat’s commitment to sharing the message of Islam. This facility prints the community’s literature and periodicals, ensuring that the teachings of Ahmadiyyat reach every corner of the globe.

The Roti Plant was particularly fascinating—a factory capable of producing thousands of rotis in mere minutes. This facility is vital in meeting the needs of the ever-growing number of visitors to Qadian, especially during Jalsa Salana.

Unified Renovations

During his visit in 2005, Hazrat Khalifatul Masih V (aba) guided the Qadian Jamaat to renovate these historical landmarks. The uniform design of red-orange bricks and concrete, paired with semi-glossy dark wood, stands out prominently across all these sites. This consistent aesthetic not only preserves the historical charm of these places but also symbolizes the unity and vision of the Jamaat.

A Pause for Prayer

In and out of cars, engrossed in the stories and sites, we didn’t realize how much time had passed. It was almost time for Zuhr prayers. We quickly returned to refresh ourselves and headed back to Darul Masih for prayers.

Each moment in Qadian is filled with spiritual blessings, and every place we visited today felt like a step closer to understanding the divine mission of the Promised Messiah (as). Alhamdulillah for this unforgettable day.
