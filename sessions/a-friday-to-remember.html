Friday, November 29, 2024

A Friday to Remember

Part I

Session 17 of 22 5 min read Qadian

How can I thank You, for all that is mine is Yours?
You have filled my home with every grace.
When Your light appeared, the darkness vanished.
Blessed is this day—Subhan Man Yarani!

It’s 4 a.m. on this cool Friday morning in Qadian. These beautiful verses by the Promised Messiah (as) echoed in my mind as I prepared for what promised to be a deeply spiritual and emotional day.

Mixed feelings overwhelmed me. It’s Friday—the most blessed day of the week—and we are in Qadian, the birthplace of Ahmadiyyat. What could be better? Alhamdulillah, we are all still healthy and in good spirits.

But today is also bittersweet as it marks our last full day in this blessed land. I recalled the accounts of companions of the Promised Messiah (as) describing how emotional their farewells to Qadian used to be. The Promised Messiah (as) would lovingly urge them to stay longer, and they would feel reluctant to leave. Today, we understand their feelings deeply. Like them, we have obligations—work, family, and school—that require us to return home.

Early Morning Devotion

Knowing this was our last day made us even more determined to make the most of it. Many of us rose early to spend time at the sacred sites within Darul Masih. We sought solitude with our Creator, thanking Him for granting us the opportunity to visit Qadian.

On my walk toward Masjid Aqsa, I passed by Darul Ziafat (Langar Khana). We had visited it the previous night and even had dinner there. The food we enjoyed at the Sarai was prepared here and sent over for us, while most other guests who come to Qadian eat their meals right at Darul Ziafat.

The admins there showed us the newly constructed "Q Wing," a section of the facility closest to Darul Masih. It is designated for VIP guests visiting Qadian and serves as a testament to the ever-growing hospitality of this sacred town. Reflecting on the dedication and seamless work of the volunteers and staff, I felt immense gratitude for everyone who ensures that Qadian remains a welcoming home for visitors from around the world.

The Langar Khana is more than just a kitchen—it is a living testament to the Promised Messiah’s (as) vision of serving humanity. Established during his lifetime, it has grown into a modern facility capable of producing thousands of meals daily. Its state-of-the-art Roti Plant can churn out countless flatbreads in minutes, meeting the needs of thousands of guests, particularly during Jalsa Salana.

A Gift of Knowledge and Exploration

At 8 a.m. sharp, the taxis were recalled for us, ready to take us on another round of exploration. Our guide, Naseer Haq Acharia Murabbi of Ammore Kharja, was there on time as ever with his welcoming smile, and Sadr Dibba Sahib joined us to oversee the day’s activities. Despite it being our last day in Qadian, the excitement to see more of this blessed town was evident in everyone.

We had two specific places on our agenda for the morning: the Roti Plant, which we hadn’t covered the previous day, and a return trip to the bookstore.

The Roti Plant was a remarkable experience. This state-of-the-art facility is a one-of-a-kind operation capable of producing thousands of rotis in minutes. It serves the ever-growing needs of the guests who visit Qadian, particularly during Jalsa Salana. Witnessing the efficiency of this plant, along with the dedication of those working there, was inspiring. It felt like a living example of how the Promised Messiah’s (as) vision of service continues to be fulfilled in the most practical and impactful ways.

After this, we made our way back to the Ahmadiyya Bookstore. Yesterday, the staff had stayed open just for us, but we hadn’t been able to browse as much as we wanted due to time constraints. Today, they once again graciously welcomed us, ensuring we had ample time to explore and purchase books.

The shelves were filled with beloved Ahmadiyya Islamic literature, all printed right here in Qadian at the Fazl-e-Umar Press. The excitement among us was palpable as we eagerly picked out books to take home—treasures of knowledge that would remind us of this spiritual journey and help us continue to grow in our faith.

We left the bookstore with bags full of books and hearts full of gratitude. Alhamdulillah, these moments were yet another testament to the blessings of Qadian. From there, we hurried back to prepare for Jumma, knowing that time in this sacred town was quickly slipping away.

A Special Moment: Calling the Adhan

My morning was more rushed than others’. I was preparing for a deeply personal and humbling experience—calling the Adhan for Jumma prayers at Masjid Aqsa. Jumma was scheduled for 1:15 p.m., and the first Adhan would be called at 12:45 p.m. I hurried to get ready and left for the masjid as others prepared for Jumma and began packing for our departure from Qadian.

My wish of calling the Adhan in Qadian was being fulfilled once again—and not just once, but thrice. Jumma prayers involve two Adhans, and I was assigned to call both.

Blessed is this day, the one in which I found my purpose.
Blessed is this day—Subhan Man Yarani!

As I stood in Masjid Aqsa waiting for the time, the realization of this moment struck me deeply. This wasn’t just about my voice echoing from the masjid—it was about being part of a spiritual legacy that connects us to the Promised Messiah (as).

The MTA Qadian team was present to capture parts of my Adhan at the masjid. Throughout our time in Qadian, MTA Qadian had been documenting our journey alongside Ghalib Ut-Hur Khan and Naem R. Mohamed from the USA. Their dedication to capturing these moments ensured that the memories of this trip would be preserved for generations to come.

An Afternoon of Reflection and Farewell

Jumma prayers at Masjid Aqsa were a deeply moving experience. The masjid was filled with the spiritual energy of hundreds of worshippers, united in prayer. After the khutba and prayers, we gathered for lunch at Sara-i-Waseem.

In the afternoon, many of us visited the Ahmadiyya Bookstore for the final time, purchasing keepsakes and beloved books printed at the Fazl-e-Umar Press. Others sought quiet moments of reflection, offering Nawafil at various sacred sites across Qadian.

This day felt like the culmination of everything we had experienced here—a blend of spirituality, history, and community. As the sun began to set, we prepared for our final evening in this sacred town, cherishing the memories and lessons we would carry with us forever.
