Friday, November 29, 2024

A Farewell Full of Gratitude

Part II

Session 18 of 22 5 min read Qadian

Our hearts were getting heavier as the hours passed. We only had a few precious moments left in this sacred town, and I couldn’t help but feel the weight of the goodbye looming ahead. I didn’t want to leave, and I knew this was the sentiment shared by most of us.

The Friday Sermon

As evening approached, we headed back to Masjid Aqsa for combined Maghrib and Ishaa prayers. Huzur’s Friday Sermon, delivered live from London, UK, was scheduled for 6:30 p.m. Announcements were made, and members gathered in the masjid, filling the space with eager anticipation.

Huzur (aba) spoke on the topic of the Treaty of Hudaibiyah, capturing the rich history of Islam and the noble character of the Holy Prophet Muhammad (sa). He described the conditions of the treaty, the apprehension felt by Hazrat Umar (ra), and how the Holy Prophet (sa) dispelled the anxiety of the Muslims. His sermon was a profound reminder of patience, trust in Allah, and the wisdom of leadership. His words resonated deeply, serving as a timely reflection on the enduring lessons from Islamic history and the responsibilities we bear as members of the Jamaat.

A Look at Qadian’s Transformation

After the sermon, we were asked to remain in the masjid to watch a documentary on the renovation work in Qadian. The documentary was both informative and inspiring, showcasing the remarkable transformation of Qadian over the years.

The narration highlighted the revelation received by the Promised Messiah (as) in 1897:

Enlarge your abode. People will come to you from very distant lands.

Tadhkirah [English], p. 382

The prophecy has been fulfilled in extraordinary ways. Qadian has undergone numerous renovations to accommodate the growing number of visitors. The most recent renovation project began in 2005, after the visit of Hazrat Khalifatul Masih V (aba). The documentary showed many before-and-after pictures of the buildings, and it was remarkable to see the transformation.

Almost every site we visited over the past three days was part of this massive effort. From the Langar Khana to the Masjid Aqsa, from the Roti Plant to the Ahmadiyya Bookstore, the renovations have preserved the historical significance of Qadian while modernizing it to meet the needs of today.

While watching, I realized the work is far from done. Qadian continues to grow and evolve under the guidance of Huzur (aba), preparing to welcome even more people from across the world.

After the documentary, we gathered for one last group photo in front of the Minaratul Masih. Standing together, we felt the weight of our collective gratitude and the bittersweet reality of bidding farewell. We said our goodbyes to Darul Masih, offering one last Salam to Masjid Mubarak and Masjid Aqsa. These sacred places, where so many prayers have been answered and where history was made, had become part of our hearts.

As we walked back to Sara-i-Waseem, the Minaratul Masih shone brightly behind us, a beacon of hope and a reminder of the incredible legacy of the Promised Messiah (as).

A Last Supper in Qadian

The food quality throughout our stay was nothing short of remarkable. Every single meal was delicious, prepared with love and care, and incredibly healthy. Over the entire trip, no one ever complained about getting sick after eating, nor did anyone grow tired of the food served at the dining hall. Each bite of roti and every dish prepared at Darul Ziafat seemed infused with thoughtfulness and effort, leaving us grateful for every meal.

The hospitality staff, mostly graduates of Jamia Ahmadiyya Qadian currently serving as murabbis, served us tirelessly. These students come not just from Qadian but from all over India and sometimes overseas. Their service was seamless, quiet, and filled with genuine affection. Tea always followed dessert, arriving just when needed. Their proactive care and love for the guests of the Promised Messiah (as) created an atmosphere that will be hard to forget.

During dinner, the air was thick with sadness. Many of us took the time to thank these gracious hosts who had been feeding and caring for us over the last few days. They served us selflessly, asking for nothing in return, and their kindness deeply touched us all.

A Special Encounter

I asked one murabbi if they liked Qadian as much as we did. To my surprise, he said, “We are sad when we have to go home for holidays. We cannot wait to return to Qadian.”

Later that evening, another murabbi from Kashmir shared a poignant moment with me. When I asked if he felt relieved when guests left Qadian, he paused and asked me which room I was staying in. It seemed unusual, but I told him.

That night, he knocked on my door with his phone in hand. On the screen was a nazm from Durr-e-Sameen by the Promised Messiah (as):

When guests come with love and affection,
It brings joy to the heart and comfort to my soul.
But when the time of farewell comes, it grieves the heart.
Blessed is this day—Subhan Man Yarani!

He said, “We love serving and helping the guests of the Promised Messiah (as). We feel honored that you have traveled from lands far away to visit this small village. But yes, we too feel sad when the time of farewell arrives.”

Our eyes welled up as we embraced. The moment was a testament to the bond of brotherhood that transcends borders, languages, and time.

It was time to finish packing and prepare for our early departure. Our buses would be ready by 5 a.m.

As we drifted off to sleep, our hearts were filled with gratitude for the countless blessings of this journey. May we carry this experience with us and let it inspire us to serve humanity and our Jamaat with renewed zeal.
